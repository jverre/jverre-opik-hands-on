# Galleries of the Alderbank Museum of Tides and Timekeeping

## The Orrery Room
The Orrery Room is on the second floor of the East Wing. Its centrepiece is a brass
planetary model built in 1783, driven by a clockwork train that is wound every morning at
10:15. The room is dim by design to protect the enamel dials, and touching the model is
not permitted. The Orrery Room closes thirty minutes before the rest of the museum.

## The Chronometer Gallery
The Chronometer Gallery fills the ground floor of the West Wing. It shows forty marine
chronometers, including the Marlowe No. 4, which kept time through a three-year voyage
to the southern ocean. A small workbench at the end of the gallery has a watchmaker on
duty from Wednesday to Saturday, and visitors may ask questions while repairs are under way.

## The Tidal Hall
The Tidal Hall is the large glass-roofed space at the centre of the museum. A 12-metre
working tide model fills with water in a six-hour cycle, matching the real tide at
Alderbank harbour. The model is drained for maintenance on the first Monday of every
month, when the hall stays open but the model is empty.

## The Lantern Cellar
The Lantern Cellar is a vaulted basement that holds fourteen lighthouse lenses and lamps.
It is reached by a spiral stair from the Tidal Hall. Because of the stair, the cellar can
only be visited on foot, and a filmed tour of it plays on a screen in the Tidal Hall for
visitors who cannot go down.

## The Children's Workshop
The Children's Workshop is on the ground floor of the East Wing, next to the cloakroom.
Drop-in sessions run at 11:00 and 14:00 on weekends and daily during school holidays.
Each session lasts forty minutes and is suited to ages five to eleven. Under-fives are
welcome when accompanied by an adult.
