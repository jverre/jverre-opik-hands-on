# Famous pieces in the collection

## The Marlowe No. 4 chronometer
The Marlowe No. 4 is a marine chronometer made in 1811 by the instrument maker Edith
Marlowe. It lost only eleven seconds during a three-year voyage and is displayed in the
Chronometer Gallery in a case that is lit for two minutes at a time to limit fading.

## The Whitby Orrery
The Whitby Orrery is a brass model of the six planets known in 1783, built by the clockmaker
Josiah Pell. It stands in the Orrery Room and is the only working example of its kind in
the country. Its mechanism is wound by hand every morning at 10:15, and visitors are
welcome to watch.

## The Fenwick Lens
The Fenwick Lens is a first-order Fresnel lighthouse lens, nearly three metres tall, taken
from the Fenwick Rock lighthouse when it was automated in 1962. It is the largest object in
the Lantern Cellar and is switched on for a few minutes each hour.

## The Harbour Log of 1847
The Harbour Log of 1847 is a handwritten record of every ship that entered Alderbank
harbour during one stormy winter. It is kept in a sealed case in the Tidal Hall and one
page is turned on the first of each month. A digital copy can be browsed on the touch
tables beside it.

## The Sandglass Wall
The Sandglass Wall is a modern installation of 600 sand timers, each set to a different
duration from ten seconds to one hour. It was commissioned in 2019 and is on the first
floor landing of the West Wing. Visitors are invited to turn a timer and watch it run.
