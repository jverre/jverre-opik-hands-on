# Copperkettle Handbook: Hop Schedules

## Bittering additions
Bittering hops go in at the start of the 60 minute boil. Use a high alpha variety and weigh them carefully: the house pale ale takes 18 g of Magnum per 20 litre batch for about 35 IBU. Every 10 minutes the hops spend out of the boil costs bitterness, so a 30 minute addition gives roughly 70 percent of the full 60 minute figure.

## Flavor additions
Flavor hops are added with 15 minutes left in the boil. Keep them to 20 to 30 g per batch of a variety like Cascade or Amarillo. Anything added earlier than 20 minutes loses most of its aroma, and anything added at flameout is better treated as a whirlpool addition.

## Whirlpool hops
For a hazy IPA, cool the wort to 80 C after the boil, add the whirlpool hops, and stir for 20 minutes before chilling. At 80 C the oils dissolve without isomerising much, so the beer gains aroma with little added bitterness. Copperkettle uses 60 g of Citra and 40 g of Mosaic for a 20 litre batch of hazy IPA. Never whirlpool above 85 C if you want a soft bitterness.

## Dry hopping
Dry hop on day 4 of fermentation, while the yeast is still active, to avoid oxygen pickup and to encourage biotransformation. Use 4 g per litre for a hazy IPA and leave the hops in for no more than 4 days; longer contact brings a grassy, vegetal taste. Bag the hops with a stainless weight so they stay submerged.
