# Copperkettle Handbook: Fermentation Troubleshooting

## Stuck fermentation
A fermentation is stuck when the gravity has not moved for 3 days and is still more than 4 points above the target. First check the temperature: if the fermenter has dropped below 16 C, warm it to 20 C and rouse the yeast gently by swirling the fermenter. Do not add more yeast until you have waited 48 hours after warming. If the gravity still sits at 1.030 or higher after that, pitch a packet of dry champagne yeast rehydrated in 30 C water.

## Diacetyl and buttery flavors
A buttery or butterscotch taste is diacetyl. It appears when the beer is packaged too early or the fermentation was cold. Run a diacetyl rest: once the gravity is within 3 points of final, raise the fermenter to 22 C for 48 hours so the yeast can clean it up, then crash cool. Taste a warmed sample before you package.

## Sulfur and egg smells
A sulfur smell during fermentation is normal for lager yeast and fades by itself in two weeks of cold conditioning. If a top-fermenting ale still smells of rotten eggs after 10 days, the yeast is stressed; check that the wort had enough oxygen at pitching and raise the temperature by 2 C.

## Signs of infection
White pellicle on the surface, a sour smell the recipe did not ask for, or ropy strands in the liquid all point to infection. Taste nothing that has a pellicle. Bleach the equipment, discard the plastic fittings and buckets that touched the batch, and treat every future batch with a full sanitation cycle. An infected batch cannot be rescued.
