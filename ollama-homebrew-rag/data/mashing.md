# Copperkettle Handbook: Mashing

## Mash temperature and body
Copperkettle brewers pick the mash temperature before anything else, because it decides how fermentable the wort is. Mash at 63 C for a dry, crisp beer such as the house saison; the beta amylase works fast at this temperature and the finished beer drops to about 1.004. Mash at 68 C for a fuller, sweeter beer such as a milk stout, which leaves roughly 1.016. The standard Copperkettle mash is 66 C for 60 minutes. Hold the temperature within 1 degree for the whole rest.

## Mash pH
Aim for a mash pH between 5.2 and 5.4, measured at room temperature with a calibrated meter after the first 15 minutes of the rest. Pale beers drift high, so add 2 ml of 80 percent lactic acid per 10 litres of mash when a reading comes in above 5.5. Dark roasted malts push the pH down by themselves and rarely need acid.

## Mash-out and sparge
After the main rest, raise the mash to 76 C for 10 minutes. This is the mash-out; it thins the wort so it runs off cleanly. Sparge with water at 77 C and never hotter, because above 80 C the grain husks release tannins that taste like dry tea. Stop collecting runnings when the gravity of the stream falls to 1.008.

## Water profile
Soft water suits lagers and pale ales. For hoppy ales, Copperkettle adds 4 g of gypsum per 10 litres of brewing water to sharpen the bitterness. For malty beers it adds 3 g of calcium chloride per 10 litres instead. Never add both in the same batch.
